_Rationale:_ The stylized fact has two parts: (1) growth can be simultaneous with reproduction, and (2) growth can also cease before reproduction begins. Both parts are well supported across the corpus. Multiple snippets describe the indeterminate vs. determinate growth dichotomy: indeterminate growth continues during the reproductive stage (snippets 1, 2, 4, 5, 14, 15, 21, 22, 24), while determinate growth ceases at or before maturity (snippets 1, 5, 7, 11). Snippet 7 explicitly notes that depending on the maturity threshold for puberty, growth may or may not proceed during reproduction, and that growth can cease before reproduction. Snippets 12 and 13 directly affirm that somatic growth can stop before/separate from reproductive investment, and that growth and reproduction can also proceed simultaneously. Snippet 8 mildly contradicts a related claim (that growth slows with onset of reproduction) but actually reinforces the idea that growth is not strictly governed by reproduction. Overall the evidence is strong and consistent with the stylized fact, with little genuine conflict.
