_Rationale:_ Multiple snippets directly support the stylized fact that energy allocation is reprioritized to pay maintenance costs first under hypoxia. Snippet 1 explicitly states the fact with primary citations (Cheung, Wei, Zhou). Snippet 10 (DEB framework applied to scallops) reaffirms that maintenance costs are fulfilled in priority per Kooijman 2010, with growth/reproduction compromised. Snippet 8 proposes a refined priority scheme splitting maintenance into essential (first priority) and non-essential fractions, and snippet 9 describes the 'pessimum' state where all energy goes to maintenance—both consistent. Snippets 3, 11, 12 from DEB theory restate the kappa-rule principle that maintenance is paid first. Snippet 16 provides empirical evidence of metabolic prioritization among demands as oxygen declines. The main partial dissent is snippet 6, which found that increasing maintenance costs was not the key bioenergetic mechanism in early-life-stage fish hypoxia responses—though this addresses elevated maintenance magnitude rather than the prioritization ordering itself. Overall the evidence is moderately strong and clearly corroborative of the prioritization principle, with only minor conflict.
