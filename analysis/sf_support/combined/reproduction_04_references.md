_Rationale:_ Multiple snippets directly and explicitly support the stylized fact. DEB-specific sources (Pecquerie et al. [9], Kooijman & Sousa [11], Sardi et al. [1]) state that offspring reserve density / egg size depends on the nutritional state of the mother (well-fed females produce offspring in good condition). Empirical works on Daphnia [6,7], copepod-style assimilate models [2], and bird/fish maternal-effects studies [16,17,19] corroborate that egg size or provisioning covaries with maternal condition or size. The main tension comes from snippets [5] and [14]: [5] notes some species produce larger eggs under poor feeding conditions (an inverse relationship in certain taxa), and [14] found no link between fecundity/egg content and nutritional metrics in lobsters. These show the covariation is not universal in sign, but they do not deny that egg size covaries with maternal nutritional status—if anything they confirm a relationship exists, just sometimes inverse. Overall the evidence is strong and consensus is corroborating, with limited and explicable conflict.
