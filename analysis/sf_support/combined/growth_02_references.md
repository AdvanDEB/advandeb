Multiple snippets explicitly define indeterminate growth as growth continuing past maturation/reproduction (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 11, 13, 14), and several document empirical cases across taxa: fish (10, 20), insects (7), lizards (15), turtles (21), kangaroos (24), plants/mollusks/crustaceans/ectothermic vertebrates (12, 18). DEB-relevant sources (Kooijman texts, snippets 3, 11) explicitly contrast indeterminate vs. determinate growth strategies. One snippet (13) even argues indeterminate growth may be the ancestral condition. A few snippets caution that indeterminate growth does not mean unlimited size (8, 9), but these refine rather than contradict the fact. No snippet contradicts the claim that many species do not stop growing after reproduction starts.
