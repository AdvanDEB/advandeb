_Rationale:_ The stylized fact that many species have a non-reproducing juvenile stage is strongly and repeatedly corroborated across the corpus. DEB-theoretic sources explicitly distinguish embryo, juvenile, and adult life stages with reproduction beginning only upon reaching a maturity threshold (Sous2007, Kooy2010, LikaKooy2011, van der Meer 2016), and several empirical/biological sources affirm that juveniles cannot or do not reproduce (snippet 21 on juvenile lizards, snippet 22 on C. elegans, snippet 11 on sticklebacks needing 1-2 years to mature). Snippet 2 appears to allow 'juvenile organisms can reproduce' but this is a modeling simplification merging juvenile/adult stages rather than a biological contradiction, and even it presupposes a maturity threshold. The few near-contradictory examples (e.g., the stoat reaching maturity before weaning in snippet 1) are explicitly framed as unusual variations, reinforcing the general rule. Overall the evidence is multiple, clear, and consistent with little genuine conflict.
