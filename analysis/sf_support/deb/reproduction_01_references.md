_Rationale:_ The corpus provides strong, multiple corroborating references for the existence of a non-reproducing juvenile stage. Snippet [15] is the explicit statement of the stylized fact ('Many species (almost all animals and plants) have a juvenile stage that does not reproduce'), and snippets [14] and [16] clearly define the juvenile stage in DEB theory as one where food is taken but resources are not yet allocated to reproduction. Snippets [10], [4], [5], [11], and [17] reinforce the embryo-juvenile-adult sequence with reproduction beginning only after a maturity/size/age threshold. Snippet [2] appears to contradict by allowing 'juveniles' to reproduce, but this is a simplified abstract model where the term lumps juvenile/adult together rather than genuine counter-evidence. Snippet [1] notes exceptions (precocious stoat maturity, unicellulars treated as juveniles), but these are framed as edge cases consistent with the qualifier 'many species.' Overall the evidence is clear and the consensus strongly corroborates the fact.
