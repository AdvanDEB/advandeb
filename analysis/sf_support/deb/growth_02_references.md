_Rationale:_ The corpus clearly establishes indeterminate growth as a recognized growth pattern in which growth continues past maturation and during the reproductive stage, directly affirming the stylized fact. Multiple snippets define indeterminate growth as growth that does not stop at/after sexual maturity (5, 6, 16) and give species examples: most fish are indeterminate growers (10), an insect with indeterminate growth (7), plants (12), and turtles/some reptiles (14). DEB sources (3, 11) explicitly contrast indeterminate growth (growth continues during reproduction) with determinate growth, treating both as real allocation strategies. The only nuance is the caveat that no organism grows truly without limit due to physiological constraints (8, 9, 15), but these refine rather than contradict the fact—they clarify that 'indeterminate' means growth does not cease at maturity, not that it is infinite. Overall the evidence strongly and consistently supports the stylized fact.
