_Rationale:_ Multiple snippets directly support the stylized fact. Snippets [2], [3], [9], [10], and [16] explicitly state that foetal structural volume/weight is proportional to cubed time, derived as V(t) = (vt/3)^3 in the standard DEB model and originally observed empirically by Huggett and Widdas (1951). Snippet [1] supports the consistency of this with the DEB model (weight doubling times increasing by factor 2^(1/3)). A few snippets ([13], [14]) instead assume exponential foetal growth, but these are simplified modeling choices in specific marine-mammal applications rather than evidence against the cubic relationship; note snippet [17] shows that under certain DEB conditions foetal length can grow approximately linearly (consistent with the cubic-weight result). The remaining snippets are topically related but neutral. Overall there is strong, repeated, mutually consistent support for the cubic-time growth pattern with only minor, application-specific deviations.
