_Rationale:_ Several snippets directly affirm the stylized fact: [8] and [17] explicitly state that fetal weight tends to increase exponentially in the middle part of gestation, with [2] employing exponential models to predict foetal fresh weights, and [6] and [9] documenting rapidly increasing foetal growth/mass during gestation. The main nuance is interspecies and stage-dependent variation: [17] notes sheep show a semilog curve convex toward the weight axis (fractional gain greater early), and [10] cites ultrasonographic evidence that human late-gestation growth is linear rather than exponential, indicating the exponential pattern is mostly a mid-gestation phenomenon rather than uniform across the whole gestation. Overall the corpus moderately and consistently supports the exponential mid-gestation growth pattern with caveats about late-gestation deceleration, so support is corroborated but qualified.
