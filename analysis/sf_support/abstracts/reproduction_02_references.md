_Rationale:_ The stylized fact has two components: an intra-specific positive size–reproduction relationship and an inter-specific negative one. A few snippets bear directly on the intra-specific positive relationship: [16] shows fecundity rises steeply with maternal body size within the Kona crab, [1] notes larger females delay maturity to produce larger offspring, and [15]/[17] touch on size-dependent fecundity within species. Snippet [8] is the most relevant to the inter-specific claim, showing offspring-related traits negatively related to body size across Daphniidae species, and [13] discusses inverse scaling of growth costs of reproduction with maturity size. However, [10] reports gamete production unrelated to body size in an insect, which weakly conflicts with the intra-specific component. Overall the corpus is dominated by sexual size dimorphism and growth-allocation papers that are only tangentially related; direct, clear evidence for BOTH limbs of the fact (especially the inter-specific decrease) is sparse and indirect, so evidence strength is weak. The little relevant evidence leans supportive but is too thin to claim corroboration, hence neutral consensus.
