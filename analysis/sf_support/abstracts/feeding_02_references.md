_Rationale:_ Multiple snippets corroborate that organisms survive, continue some reproduction, and adapt during starvation. Several report measurable starvation survival times (rotifers [5,6], lace bug [15], Drosophila [8,10]) and describe physiological/metabolic adaptations (insects [4], C. elegans [11,16]) and trade-offs between reproduction and starvation resistance, all consistent with the fact that organisms persist for some time without food and can allocate residual resources to reproduction (e.g., Daphnia storage/reproduction trade-offs [2,17]). No snippet contradicts the fact. However, none explicitly demonstrate continued growth, and most evidence centers on survival and reproductive trade-offs rather than the full triad (reproduce, grow, survive), so support is moderate rather than strong. Overall the direction is clearly corroborative with little conflict.
