_Rationale:_ The corpus contains multiple direct and clearly relevant references supporting the stylized fact. Several snippets explicitly affirm Bergmann's rule and the related temperature-size rule (TSR): gliders are larger in colder southern ranges (1), ectotherms grow larger at low temperatures (4, 6, 9), turtles obey Bergmann's rule (11), and frog body size varies with temperature/aridity (14). The temperature-size rule ('hotter is smaller') is corroborated repeatedly. There is some genuine conflict and nuance: arthropod studies note converse Bergmann clines driven by season length (2, 3, 13), some reptiles/snakes counter the rule (11 notes snakes/lizards do so), and Pyrgomorphidae show larger females in warmer regions (15). However, these contradictions are framed as known exceptions rather than refutations, and the overall weight of evidence corroborates the pattern, especially for endotherms and many ectotherms. Thus evidence is strong and consensus leans toward corroboration despite documented exceptions.
