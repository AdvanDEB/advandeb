**Scope:** Reproduction abstracts  
**Category:** Hypoxia / DO  
**Stylized fact:** Above the critical saturation, compensatory mechanisms compensate the O2 reduction to maintain respiration and sustain the standard metabolic rate. These responses can lead to increased O2 consumption (e.g. ventilation rate, heartbeat, mobility).

_Rationale:_ The stylized fact concerns compensatory mechanisms (increased ventilation, heartbeat, mobility, convective transport) that maintain respiration and standard metabolic rate above a critical O2 saturation. Several snippets bear indirectly on this: [15] documents cardiorespiratory adjustments and elevated normoxic MO2 after hypoxic acclimation in cod; [16] describes convective oxygen transport increasing to maintain aerobic metabolism in diving seals; [11] explicitly notes glycolysis compensating (marginally) for low oxygen; [3], [5], [7], [12] discuss critical O2 thresholds (O2crit/pejus) and aerobic scope but largely from a thermal-tolerance angle rather than directly demonstrating compensatory O2 uptake above the critical point. [9] and [17] touch on ventilatory responses. No snippet directly and clearly demonstrates increased O2 consumption via ventilation/heartbeat/mobility specifically to defend SMR above critical saturation, and none contradicts the fact. Overall there is moderate, scattered support consistent with the concept of compensatory regulation, but the evidence is indirect and from heterogeneous systems, so support is moderate with insufficient targeted confirmation.
