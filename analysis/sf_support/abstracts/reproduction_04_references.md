_Rationale:_ Several snippets directly support the stylized fact that egg size/initial reserve covaries with maternal nutritional status. The strongest are [16] (Northern Lapwings: maternal mass and condition positively correlated with egg volume, with condition depending on food availability), [15] (Yellow Dung Fly: maternal adult feeding/nutritional status strongly affects egg size and reproductive output), and [14] (Sociable Weaver: egg mass varies with allocation, modulated by helper-mediated resource availability). Others ([3],[5],[6],[9]) provide moderate corroborating context linking nutritional/resource status to egg provisioning. Snippet [2] is partly contradictory, finding no relationship between fecundity/nutritional metrics and reproduction in lobsters, though it concerns fecundity more than egg size per se. Many snippets are neutral, focusing on egg-size effects on offspring or female-size–fecundity relationships rather than maternal nutritional status. Overall the corpus moderately and consistently supports the fact, with little direct contradiction.
